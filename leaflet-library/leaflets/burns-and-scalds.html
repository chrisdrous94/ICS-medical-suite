Burns and scalds

Your child had a burn or scald today. The size, depth, location and cause help determine what care is needed.

Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

What we did

School staff can mark the care given on the printed leaflet.

  • Started cooling the burn with running water.
  • Removed loose clothing or jewellery near the burn.
  • Covered the cooled burn or arranged medical help.

Incident time and additional notes

Home care

  • Cool a heat burn under cool running water for 20 minutes.
  • Gently remove nearby clothing or jewellery that is loose and leave stuck material in place.
  • Keep the rest of the child warm.
  • Once cooled, you can lay cling film loosely over the burn while arranging care.
  • Avoid wrapping it around a limb.
  • Keep ice, creams, oils and adhesive dressings away from the burn.

Caring for a small burn after cooling

  • Leave blisters intact.
  • Follow any dressing instructions you were given.
  • Ask for advice if the burn seems to be getting worse or you are concerned about how it is healing.

Red flags

  • Get emergency care for a very large or deep burn, a burn on the face, genitals or bottom, or one caused by a chemical or electricity.
  • Call 112 for breathing trouble after a fire or smoke exposure.
  • Seek urgent advice if you are unsure how serious a child’s burn is.
