Insect bites and stings

Your child had an insect bite or sting today. Some swelling and itching near the bite are common. Watch for allergic symptoms and signs of infection.

Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

What we did

School staff can mark the care given on the printed leaflet.

  • Checked the site of the bite or sting.
  • Washed the area with soap and water.
  • Applied a wrapped cold pack.

Incident time and additional notes

Home care

  • If a bee stinger is visible, scrape it sideways out of the skin.
  • Wash the area with soap and water.
  • Use a cold pack wrapped in cloth to help with swelling and discourage scratching.

Spotting signs of infection

  • Some local itching and swelling are common.
  • Ask for medical advice if the area becomes increasingly hot, painful or swollen, if pus appears, or if your child develops a fever.
  • Tell the clinician if the bite was from a tick and a spreading round rash develops.
Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

Red flags

  • Call 112 if there is breathing trouble, lip or tongue swelling, or collapse.
  • Seek urgent advice for a sting in the mouth or throat or near the eye, or if your child has been stung several times.
