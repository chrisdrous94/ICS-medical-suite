Severe allergic reaction

Your child showed signs of a possible allergic reaction. A severe reaction needs emergency hospital care, even if symptoms improve.

Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

What we did

School staff can mark the care given on the printed leaflet.

  • Called 112 and reported suspected anaphylaxis.
  • Gave adrenaline using an auto-injector.
  • Stayed with your child and monitored their breathing.

Incident time and additional notes

Home care

  • Keep the child lying down.
  • If breathing is difficult, they may raise their shoulders or sit up slowly.
  • Keep them from standing or walking, even if they seem better.
  • If symptoms have not improved after five minutes, use a second auto-injector if available.
  • Follow the device instructions and tell ambulance staff when each dose was given.

After the reaction

  • Your child needs hospital assessment even if they improve.
  • Afterwards, share the updated allergy plan with the school and check that prescribed spare devices are available and in date.
Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

Red flags

  • Use the child’s adrenaline auto-injector immediately if available and call 112.
  • Say “suspected anaphylaxis.” Look for sudden throat or tongue swelling, difficulty breathing or swallowing, faintness, or a child becoming floppy or unresponsive.
  • A rash can also occur.
