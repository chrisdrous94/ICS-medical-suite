Vomiting and diarrhoea

Your child had vomiting or diarrhoea today. The main thing to watch at home is whether they are drinking and passing urine normally.

Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

What we did

School staff can mark the care given on the printed leaflet.

  • Noted the symptoms and when they started.
  • Offered small sips of fluid if your child could drink.
  • Contacted a parent or carer.

Incident time and additional notes

Home care

  • Offer small, frequent sips rather than large drinks.
  • Signs of dehydration include a dry mouth, dark urine or fewer wet nappies.
  • A pharmacist or paediatrician can advise about oral rehydration solution.
  • Fruit juice and fizzy drinks can make diarrhoea worse.

Hydration and returning to school

  • Wash hands with soap and water, clean shared bathroom surfaces and avoid sharing towels.
  • Check the school’s return policy.
  • NHS guidance advises waiting at least 48 hours after the last episode of vomiting or diarrhoea.
Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

Red flags

  • Get urgent advice if your child keeps vomiting and cannot keep fluids down, passes much less urine or has bloody diarrhoea.
  • Go to an emergency department for green vomit or sudden severe tummy pain.
  • Call 112 if your child is unresponsive or struggling to breathe.
