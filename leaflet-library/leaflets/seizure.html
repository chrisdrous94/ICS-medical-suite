Seizure or fit

Your child had an episode that may have been a seizure. The timing, what staff saw, and how your child recovered can help their clinician.

Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

What we did

School staff can mark the care given on the printed leaflet.

  • Timed the episode.
  • Cleared hazards and protected their head.
  • Stayed with your child during recovery and followed their care plan if available.

Incident time and additional notes

Home care

  • Note when the seizure starts.
  • Move nearby hazards away and cushion the head.
  • Only move the child if they are in danger.
  • Keep objects and fingers out of their mouth and avoid holding them down.
  • When the shaking stops, roll them onto their side if they are breathing and stay with them until they recover.
  • Follow their individual emergency plan if you have been trained to do so.
  • Wait until they have fully recovered before offering food or drink.

What to record and share

  • Tell the clinician how long it lasted, what movements you noticed and how long recovery took.
  • If your child has a seizure plan, let the school know about updates.

Call 112

Red flags

  • Call 112 for a first seizure, breathing problems afterwards, serious injury, repeated seizures without recovery, or a seizure that lasts longer than usual.
  • If you do not know how long their seizures usually last, call if it lasts more than five minutes.
