Nosebleeds

Your child had a nosebleed today. It can restart, particularly if they blow or pick their nose soon afterward.

Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

What we did

School staff can mark the care given on the printed leaflet.

  • Helped your child sit upright and lean forward.
  • Helped apply continuous pressure to the soft part of the nose.
  • Timed the bleeding and checked whether it stopped.

Incident time and additional notes

Home care

  • Sit your child upright with their head forward.
  • Ask them to breathe through their mouth and spit out blood that collects there.
  • Squeeze the soft part of their nose continuously for 10 to 15 minutes.
  • Use a timer so you avoid letting go to check.

Reducing the chance of another bleed

  • During the next 24 hours, avoid nose blowing, nose picking, hot drinks and strenuous play.
  • Ask your child’s doctor for advice if nosebleeds keep coming back, if they have a clotting condition or if a child under two has had a nosebleed.
Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

Red flags

  • Go to an emergency department if bleeding continues beyond 10 to 15 minutes, is heavy, follows a blow to the head or makes your child weak or dizzy.
  • Call 112 if they have serious breathing trouble.
