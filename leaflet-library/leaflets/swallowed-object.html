Swallowed battery, magnet or object

Your child may have swallowed an object. Button batteries and magnets need emergency assessment even if your child seems well.

Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

What we did

School staff can mark the care given on the printed leaflet.

  • Checked what the object might have been.
  • Recorded when it may have been swallowed.
  • Arranged urgent medical assessment.

Incident time and additional notes

Home care

  • Tell the clinician what was swallowed, when, and whether more than one item could be missing.
  • Bring packaging or a matching item if it is nearby.
  • A clinician can decide whether an examination or X-ray is needed.

Signs to watch for after assessment

  • If your child is sent home, follow the hospital’s instructions.
  • Return urgently for trouble swallowing, neck or chest pain, vomiting, tummy pain or blood in vomit or stools.
  • Call 112 if breathing becomes difficult.
Life-threatening emergency? Call an ambulance in Cyprus.
Call 112

Red flags

  • Go straight to an emergency department for any suspected button battery or magnet, even if your child looks well.
  • Call 112 if they cannot breathe, speak or cough effectively.
  • Seek prompt medical advice for other swallowed objects.
